\section{Algorithm Description} \label{sec:intro}

Hash-based signature is one of the most promising candidates for (and perhaps the most conservative approach to)  post-quantum digital signatures. An advantage of hash-based signatures is that its (classical as well as quantum) security strength is better understood (and easier to evaluate) than other candidates, by solely relying on the idealized hardness\footnote{The design philosophy of symmetric primitives (including hash functions) is that they should only admit generic attacks, otherwise the design is considered to be flawed.} of the cryptographic hash functions.  

\paragraph{Stateful signatures.} Ralph Merkle proposed a hash-based signature \cite{C:Merkle89a} that builds upon Lamport's one-time signature (OTS) \cite{Lamport79}. The recent efforts towards improving stateful signatures lead to the eXtended Merkle Signature Scheme (XMSS)~\cite{hulsing2018xmss} and the Leighton-Micali Signature (LMS)~\cite{mcgrew2019leighton},  standardized by NIST~\cite{cooper2020recommendation} and IETF.

\paragraph{Stateless signatures.} In a typical stateful signature, e.g., Merkle's signature scheme (MSS), the signer keeps track of which private key of the OTS has been used to avoid security issues from subsequent reuse. This is however not always possible in many practical scenarios. Goldreich proposed a stateless hash-based signature construction~\cite{C:Goldreich86a,Goldreich04} which removes the need for maintaining a local state but results in prohibitively large signatures. Recently, this line of research has renewed interest. By incorporating the hypertree structure, SPHINCS~\cite{EC:BHHLNP15} offered a practical instantiation of the Goldreich-style stateless hash-based signature. SPHINCS serves as a basis for subsequent works driven by the NIST PQC standardization process, including Gravity-SPHINCS~\cite{RSA:AumEnd18}, SPHINCS-Simpira~\cite{EPRINT:GueMou17}, and \SPHINCSP\ \cite{CCS:BHKNRS19}. More recent optimizations and analyses include SPHINCS+C~\cite{SP:HKRY23}, forced-pruning techniques for shortening SPHINCS+-style few-time components~\cite{EPRINT:AbrKat25}, improved size-time tradeoffs in hypertree-based settings~\cite{C:KhoKudWag25}, variable-height/chain-length framework extensions~\cite{EPRINT:QinSun25}, and the SPHINCS-alpha work revisiting constant-sum Winternitz one-time signatures with applications to SPHINCS+ and XMSS~\cite{optimalwots}. NIST has also published the finalized federal standard for stateless hash-based signatures (SLH-DSA) in FIPS 205~\cite{NIST:FIPS205}.

\paragraph{Summary of our Work.}
We propose \SPHINCSA, a stateless hash-based signature scheme, which improves upon the state-of-the-art stateless hash-based signature scheme \SPHINCSP, while preserving the core elements that made the original \SPHINCSP a standout project. 

The construction combines three algorithmic changes.  First, \WOTSCS provides
a size-optimal constant-sum encoding for Winternitz-type one-time signatures;
no DAG-based construction is known to offer better size efficiency for this
component~\cite{optimalwots}.  Second, BPORS+FP replaces the bottom few-time
layer and uses bucket thinning with a fixed authentication-node budget to reduce
the few-time signature payload~\cite{bucketthinning}.  Third, Kylin allows
diverse hypertree layer heights during parameter selection.

\cref{tab:slh-kylin-small-comp} gives a compact comparison against the
SLH-DSA small parameter sets.  Signature sizes are in bytes, and signing
and verification costs are reported as design-level hash-call proxies.  For
the principal metrics shown there (signing time, verification time, and
signature size), Kylin is strictly better than the corresponding SLH-DSA row at
both the 128-bit and 256-bit levels.

\paragraph{Diverse Layer Heights in the Hypertree.}
Kylin also uses a flexible hypertree profile with per-layer heights $(h_0,\ldots,h_{d-1})$ rather than requiring all $d$ layers to have the same height. This removes the unnecessary divisibility restriction $h \bmod d = 0$ and lets the parameter search choose balanced layer-height vectors whenever the total hypertree height $h$ is not divisible by the number of layers.

The security analysis therefore follows the tight \SPHINCSP proof structure
while replacing the WOTS checksum argument by the constant-sum antichain
argument and replacing the FORS cover calculation by the BPORS+FP cover term.
Based on this adapted analysis, we estimate the concrete generic-security
exponents and choose parameters for the packaged profiles.

To facilitate a direct package-level view, \cref{tab:comp tiny} reports the hash-call proxies and key/signature sizes for \texttt{Kylin-128}, \texttt{Kylin-256}, and \texttt{Kylin-512}. In particular, the table includes key-generation, signing, and verification hash-call metrics under the frozen packaged parameter sets.

We refer to more detailed comparisons for the full spectrum of parameter choices in \cref{sec:performance}.
As summarized in \cref{tab:kylin-package-bench}, the packaged Kylin instances provide the current repository-local performance profile for key generation, signing, and verification. For specific scenarios where verification time is critical, the parameters can be re-tuned towards faster verification.
