\section{Security Statements and Analyses}
\label{sec:security-claims}

\providecommand{\InSec}[1]{\mathbf{InSec}^{\mathsf{#1}}}
\providecommand{\csenc}{\mathsf{cs\text{-}wots\text{-}encode}}
\providecommand{\Chain}{\mathsf{chain}}
\providecommand{\THFColl}{\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{H}}}

This section gives the security claim and proof outline for the Kylin signature
scheme specified in \cref{sec:spx-core}.  The reduction follows the tight
\SPHINCSP proof framework of H\"ulsing and Kudinov~\cite{AC:HulKud22}, while
replacing the WOTS+ checksum argument by the constant-sum antichain argument of
\WOTSCS~\cite{optimalwots}, and replacing the FORS few-time layer by
BPORS+FP~\cite{bucketthinning,EPRINT:AbrKat25}.  The recommended NGCC-aligned parameter
families are recorded in \cref{tab:ngcc-parameters}, and the packaged ICCS
hash/XOF interface is described in \cref{sec:hashfunctions}.

\subsection{Security Model and Notation}
\label{sec:proof-model}

Throughout this section, $n$ denotes the Kylin security parameter in bytes and
$N=8n$ denotes the corresponding bit length.  All basic hash-chain values,
secret-key elements, public-key elements, and Merkle-tree nodes are $n$-byte
strings, i.e., elements of $\{0,1\}^{N}$.  The Winternitz parameter is $w$, the
\WOTSCS chain count is $\ell=\wotslen$, the BPORS+FP parameters are
$(K,B,t,k,m_{\max})$, and the hypertree has total height $h$ split into
per-layer heights $(h_0,\ldots,h_{d-1})$ with $\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}h_i=h$.

The tweakable hash-function collection is parameterized by the public seed
$\pk.\seed$ and by 32-byte addresses \ADRS.  In this collection, $\hashF$ maps
one $n$-byte input to one $n$-byte output, $\hashH$ maps two $n$-byte inputs to
one $n$-byte output, and $\Th_r$ compresses $r$ $n$-byte inputs to one
$n$-byte output.  Kylin also uses the key-generation PRF \PRF, the message
randomization PRF \PRFmsg, and the message digest function \hashmsg.

For a primitive or scheme $X$, $\InSec{sec}(X;\tau,q)$ denotes the maximum
advantage of any adversary of running time at most $\tau$ making at most $q$
relevant oracle or target queries in the indicated security experiment.  The
analysis assumes the standard post-quantum model used in the \SPHINCSP proof:
the adversary may perform quantum computation, while the keyed signing and
hashing oracles in the reduction are accessed classically.

Kylin relies on the following properties.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \PRF and \PRFmsg are pseudorandom functions.
    \item \hashmsg satisfies interleaved target subset resilience (ITSR).
    \item $\hashF$, as a member of the tweakable-hash collection, satisfies
    single-function multi-target undetectability (SM-UD), target-collision
    resistance (SM-TCR), preimage resistance (SM-PRE), and decisional
    second-preimage resistance (SM-DSPR) for distinct tweaks.
    \item $\hashH$, $\Th_K$, and $\Th_\ell$ satisfy SM-TCR for distinct tweaks.
\end{itemize}

The distinct-tweak condition is enforced by the address scheme in
\cref{sec:addr}.  The reductions below only place targets at addresses that
are fixed by a virtual BPORS+FP, XMSS, or \WOTSCS instance; all other evaluations
are simulated using the same tweakable-hash collection.

\subsection{Security of CS-WOTS-TW}
\label{sec:proof-cswots}

The one-time signature layer used in the proof is CS-WOTS-TW: the WOTS-TW
scheme of~\cite{AC:HulKud22} with the \WOTSCS constant-sum encoding in place
of the WOTS+ message-plus-checksum encoding.  This subsection records the
encoding property and the single-instance and multi-instance reductions.

\subsubsection{The Constant-Sum Antichain Property}
\label{sec:proof-antichain}

Let $[w]=\{0,\ldots,w-1\}$, and define the product partial order on
$[w]^\ell$ by
\[
    \mathbf{x}\preceq\mathbf{y}
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    x_i\le y_i\text{ for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}.
\]
For an integer $s$, define the constant-sum level
\[
    \mathcal{C}_s
    =\left\{\mathbf{x}\in [w]^\ell:\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_i=s\right\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Constant-sum antichain]
\label{lem:proof-antichain}
For every $s$, the set $\mathcal{C}_s$ is an antichain in
$([w]^\ell,\preceq)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}_s$, $\mathbf{x}\preceq
\mathbf{y}$, and $\mathbf{x}\ne\mathbf{y}$.  Then at least one coordinate is
strictly smaller in $\mathbf{x}$ while no coordinate is larger, so
$\sum_i x_i<\sum_i y_i$, contradicting
$\sum_i x_i=\sum_i y_i=s$.
\end{proof}

Let $D_{r,m}$ be the number of $r$-tuples in $\{0,\ldots,w-1\}^r$ whose
coordinates sum to $m$.  The dynamic program used by the encoder is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-drm}
    D_{0,0}=1,\qquad
    D_{0,m}=0\;(m\ne0),\qquad
    D_{r,m}=\sum_{i=0}^{w-1}D_{r-1,m-i},
\end{equation}
with $D_{r,m}=0$ outside the interval $0\le m\le r(w-1)$.  The Kylin parameter
$\ell$ is selected so that, for
$s^\star=\lfloor\ell(w-1)/2\rfloor$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-len-condition}
    D_{\ell,s^\star}\ge 2^N.
\end{equation}
The encoder ranks the first $2^N$ elements of $\mathcal{C}_{s^\star}$ using
the recurrence in \cref{eq:proof-drm}.

\begin{lemma}[\WOTSCS forgery direction]
\label{lem:proof-cswots-direction}
Let $M\ne M'$ be two $N$-bit inputs to the \WOTSCS encoder, and let
$\mathbf{b}=\csenc(M)$ and $\mathbf{b}'=\csenc(M')$.  Then there exist indices
$j$ and $j'$ such that $b'_j<b_j$ and $b_{j'}<b'_{j'}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both encoded vectors lie in the same constant-sum level
$\mathcal{C}_{s^\star}$.  By \cref{lem:proof-antichain}, they are
incomparable.  If no coordinate satisfied $b'_j<b_j$, then
$\mathbf{b}\preceq\mathbf{b}'$, a contradiction.  The symmetric argument gives
an index $j'$ with $b_{j'}<b'_{j'}$.
\end{proof}

This lemma is the only place where the Kylin proof differs from the WOTS-TW
part of the tight \SPHINCSP proof.  In WOTS+, the checksum guarantees that a
changed message decreases at least one chain index.  In \WOTSCS, the same
``decreasing chain'' needed for preimage extraction follows directly from the
antichain property.

\subsubsection{Single-Instance Game Proof}
\label{sec:proof-cswots-single}

\begin{theorem}[Single-instance CS-WOTS-TW security]
\label{thm:proof-cswots-single}
Let $N=8n$, let $w=\operatorname{poly}(N)$, and let CS-WOTS-TW use $\ell$
chains with the encoder above.  If \PRF is a PRF and $\hashF$ satisfies SM-UD,
SM-TCR, and SM-PRE for distinct tweaks, then
\begin{align}
\InSec{EU\text{-}naCMA}(\mathsf{CS\text{-}WOTS\text{-}TW};\tau,1)
\le{}&
    \InSec{prf}(\PRF;\tau,\ell)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashF;\tau,\ell w) \notag\\
&   +\InSec{sm\text{-}pre}(\hashF;\tau,\ell)
    +w\cdot\InSec{sm\text{-}ud}(\hashF;\tau,\ell),
\label{eq:proof-cswots-single}
\end{align}
up to the usual additive simulation time for at most $\ell w$ evaluations of
$\hashF$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}_1,\mathcal{A}_2)$ be a one-time EU-naCMA
adversary.  In the real game $\mathsf{G}_1$, $\mathcal{A}_1$ chooses one
message $M$, the challenger generates one CS-WOTS-TW key pair under a context
$(\pk.\seed,\ADRS)$, returns a signature on $M$, and $\mathcal{A}_2$ wins by
outputting a valid signature on $M'\ne M$.

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_1\to\mathsf{G}_2$: PRF replacement.}
In $\mathsf{G}_2$, the $\ell$ secret chain starts
$\PRF(\sk.\seed,\ADRS_i)$ are replaced by independent uniform $N$-bit strings.
The address scheme makes the $\ell$ PRF inputs distinct, hence
\[
    \left|\Pr[\mathsf{G}_1]-\Pr[\mathsf{G}_2]\right|
    \le \InSec{prf}(\PRF;\tau,\ell).
\]

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_2\to\mathsf{G}_3$: SM-UD hybrid.}
Let $\mathbf{b}=\csenc(M)$.  In $\mathsf{G}_3$, the signature elements are
generated from random hidden values one step before the revealed chain
positions: for $b_i>0$, choose $u_i\leftarrow\{0,1\}^N$ and set
$\sigma_i=\hashF(\pk.\seed,T_{i,b_i-1},u_i)$; for $b_i=0$, set
$\sigma_i=u_i$.  The public key is then completed from these signature
positions exactly as in verification.  A standard hybrid over the at most
$w-1$ chain positions distinguishes real chain values from random values using
$\ell$ SM-UD targets per hybrid step, and therefore
\[
    \left|\Pr[\mathsf{G}_2]-\Pr[\mathsf{G}_3]\right|
    \le w\cdot\InSec{sm\text{-}ud}(\hashF;\tau,\ell).
\]

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_3\to\mathsf{G}_4$: excluding chain collisions.}
Game $\mathsf{G}_4$ aborts if the forgery $(M',\sigma')$ contains an index
$i$ with $b'_i<b_i$ for which
\[
    \Chain_{b'_i,b_i-b'_i}(\sigma'_i,i,\pk.\seed,\ADRS)=\sigma_i.
\]
If this event occurs, the adversarial chain and the simulated honest chain
converge below the signed position.  Taking the first point at which the two
paths differ but have the same image gives a valid SM-TCR solution for one of
at most $\ell w$ targeted chain calls.  Thus
\[
    \left|\Pr[\mathsf{G}_3]-\Pr[\mathsf{G}_4]\right|
    \le \InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashF;\tau,\ell w).
\]

\paragraph{Bounding $\mathsf{G}_4$: SM-PRE extraction.}
If $\mathcal{A}$ wins in $\mathsf{G}_4$, then
\cref{lem:proof-cswots-direction} gives an index $j$ with $b'_j<b_j$.  The
abort condition tells us that the forged chain has not already reached the
signed value $\sigma_j$ at position $b_j$, while verification tells us that it
does reach the same chain head.  Hence the value
\[
    \Chain_{b'_j,b_j-b'_j-1}(\sigma'_j,j,\pk.\seed,\ADRS)
\]
is a preimage of the SM-PRE target
$\sigma_j=\hashF(\pk.\seed,T_{j,b_j-1},u_j)$.  The extracted value is not the
hidden preimage $u_j$, otherwise the paths would have met before the position
excluded in $\mathsf{G}_4$.  Placing one SM-PRE challenge at each signed chain
position yields
\[
    \Pr[\mathsf{G}_4]\le
    \InSec{sm\text{-}pre}(\hashF;\tau,\ell).
\]
Combining the game transitions proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Multi-Instance Security}
\label{sec:proof-cswots-multi}

In Kylin, many CS-WOTS-TW instances share the same public seed but are
separated by addresses.  We write $D$ for the number of one-time instances in
this multi-instance experiment, avoiding the symbol $d$ reserved for hypertree
layers.

\begin{theorem}[Multi-instance CS-WOTS-TW security]
\label{thm:proof-cswots-multi}
Under the assumptions of \cref{thm:proof-cswots-single}, for $D$ addressed
CS-WOTS-TW instances sharing the same public seed,
\begin{align}
\InSec{D\text{-}EU\text{-}naCMA}(\mathsf{CS\text{-}WOTS\text{-}TW};\tau,D)
\le{}&
    \InSec{prf}(\PRF;\tau,D\ell)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,D\ell w) \notag\\
&   +\InSec{sm\text{-}pre}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,D\ell)
    +w\cdot\InSec{sm\text{-}ud}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,D\ell).
\label{eq:proof-cswots-multi}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Run the four games of \cref{thm:proof-cswots-single} simultaneously over all
$D$ addressed instances.  The PRF replacement uses $D\ell$ distinct inputs.
The SM-UD hybrid still has at most $w-1$ chain-position hybrids, but each
hybrid now contains $D\ell$ targets.  The SM-TCR placement covers at most
$D\ell w$ chain calls.  Finally, if the adversary forges in instance $r$,
\cref{lem:proof-cswots-direction} gives a decreasing chain coordinate in that
same instance, and the SM-PRE extraction uses one of the $D\ell$ placed
targets.  All non-challenge evaluations are simulated through the
tweakable-hash collection, and address separation ensures disjoint tweaks.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hypertree Security of Kylin}
\label{sec:proof-kylin-ht}

\subsubsection{Proof-Oriented Hypertree Description}
\label{sec:proof-ht-description}

The full Kylin signature scheme is specified in \cref{sec:spx-core}.  For the
security proof, the relevant structure is the following.  The signer first
computes
\[
    R\gets\PRFmsg(\sk.\prf,\mathsf{opt},M),
    \qquad
    (\md,\mathsf{idx}_{\mathsf{tree}},\mathsf{idx}_{\mathsf{leaf}})
    \gets\hashmsg(R,\pk.\seed,\pk.\root,M).
\]
The digest \md is signed by BPORS+FP.  The recovered BPORS+FP public key is
then signed by the bottom XMSS tree of the hypertree; each higher XMSS layer
signs the root of the layer below.

Layer $i$ has height $h_i$.  It therefore contributes one \WOTSCS signature and
one authentication path of $h_i$ $n$-byte nodes to each hypertree signature.
The number of XMSS trees at layer $i$ is
\[
    2^{\,h-\sum_{j=0}^{i}h_j},
\]
so the top layer contains one tree.  The total number of hypertree leaves is
$2^h$, and the total authentication-path size across all layers is
$hn$ bytes, independent of the distribution of the $h_i$.

\subsubsection{Reduction Theorem}
\label{sec:proof-kylin-reduction}

\begin{theorem}[EUF-CMA security of Kylin]
\label{thm:proof-kylin}
Let Kylin use parameters $n,w,h,d,(h_0,\ldots,h_{d-1}),\ell$ and BPORS+FP
parameters $(K,B,t,k,m_{\max})$ as specified above.  For any adversary making
at most $q_s$ signing queries,
\begin{align}
\InSec{EU\text{-}CMA}(\mathsf{Kylin};\tau,q_s)
\le{}&
    \InSec{prf}(\PRF;\tau,q_1)
    +\InSec{prf}(\PRFmsg;\tau,q_s)
    +\InSec{itsr}(\hashmsg;\tau,q_s) \notag\\
&   +w\cdot\InSec{sm\text{-}ud}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,q_2)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}pre}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,q_2) \notag\\
&   +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,q_3+q_7)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashH\in\THFColl;\tau,q_4) \notag\\
&   +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\Th_K\in\THFColl;\tau,q_5)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\Th_\ell\in\THFColl;\tau,q_6) \notag\\
&   +3\cdot\InSec{sm\text{-}tcr}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,q_8)
    +\InSec{sm\text{-}dspr}(\hashF\in\THFColl;\tau,q_8),
\label{eq:proof-kylin-bound}
\end{align}
where the target counts can be bounded by
\begin{align*}
q_1 &< 2^{h+1}(K2^B t+\ell),&
q_2 &< 2^{h+1}\ell,&
q_3 &< 2^{h+1}\ell w,&
q_4 &< 2^{h+1}K2^B(2t+2),\\
q_5 &< 2^h,&
q_6 &< 2^{h+1},&
q_7 &< 2^{h+1}K2^B t,&
q_8 &< 2^hK2^B t.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof adapts the tight \SPHINCSP game sequence of~\cite{AC:HulKud22} to
the \WOTSCS and BPORS+FP layers.  The hypertree, PRF, message-randomization,
and tweakable-hash games are unchanged in structure; the target counts change
because the bottom few-time layer now contains $K2^B$ child PORS trees per
bottom key and because \WOTSCS uses $\ell$ chains.

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_0$: real EUF-CMA experiment.}
The adversary receives a real Kylin public key and may ask for up to $q_s$
signatures.  It wins by outputting a valid signature on a message that was not
queried.

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_0\to\mathsf{G}_1$: replace \PRF.}
All secret values derived from $\PRF(\sk.\seed,\cdot)$ in BPORS+FP and in the
\WOTSCS instances are replaced by independent uniform values.  The number of
possible targeted secret values in the virtual structure is bounded by
$q_1<2^{h+1}(K2^Bt+\ell)$, giving the first PRF term in
\cref{eq:proof-kylin-bound}.

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_1\to\mathsf{G}_2$: replace \PRFmsg.}
The message randomizer \PRFmsg is replaced by a random function on the at most
$q_s$ signing queries.  This gives the
$\InSec{prf}(\PRFmsg;\tau,q_s)$ term.

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_2\to\mathsf{G}_3$: ITSR of \hashmsg.}
The game aborts if the forgery avoids introducing a fresh BPORS+FP child leaf
while still corresponding to an unqueried message.  This is exactly the event controlled by
interleaved target subset resilience of \hashmsg, giving
$\InSec{itsr}(\hashmsg;\tau,q_s)$.

\paragraph{Games $\mathsf{G}_{3.1}$--$\mathsf{G}_{3.4}$: excluding hash
collisions outside WOTS.}
The remaining successful forgery is decomposed by the first place where the
verification computation diverges from the honestly generated virtual
structure.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A second preimage in a BPORS+FP child tree, BPORS+FP outer tree, or
    XMSS binary tree reduces to SM-TCR of $\hashH$ with at most
    $q_4<2^{h+1}K2^B(2t+2)$ targets.
    \item A second preimage in the compression of the $K$ BPORS+FP outer roots
    reduces to SM-TCR of $\Th_K$ with at most $q_5<2^h$ targets.
    \item A second preimage in the compression of a \WOTSCS public key reduces
    to SM-TCR of $\Th_\ell$ with at most $q_6<2^{h+1}$ targets.
    \item A second preimage in a BPORS+FP child leaf computation outside the
    WOTS instances reduces to SM-TCR of $\hashF$ with at most
    $q_7<2^{h+1}K2^Bt$ targets.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Game $\mathsf{G}_{3.4}\to\mathsf{G}_4$: WOTS forgery.}
Once the above collision events are excluded, any remaining forgery in the
hypertree must contain a valid new CS-WOTS-TW signature for one of fewer than
$2^{h+1}$ addressed one-time instances.  Applying
\cref{thm:proof-cswots-multi} with $D<2^{h+1}$ gives the SM-UD, SM-PRE, and
SM-TCR terms with target counts $q_2$ and $q_3$.

\paragraph{Final game: second BPORS+FP child leaf case.}
The only remaining case is the analogous ``second leaf'' case for BPORS+FP:
the forgery contains a fresh child leaf value that verifies under the same
child and outer paths but is not the expected leaf.  As in the tight
\SPHINCSP proof, this reduces to three SM-TCR placements and one SM-DSPR term
for $\hashF$, with $q_8<2^hK2^Bt$ targets.  Summing all transition losses proves
\cref{eq:proof-kylin-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The height vector $(h_0,\ldots,h_{d-1})$ affects running time and memory through
the cost of generating individual XMSS trees, but not the reduction structure.
The security target counts above depend on the total number of virtual leaves,
WOTS instances, BPORS+FP child leaves, and authentication-path nodes, all
bounded using the total height $h$ and the BPORS+FP parameters.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Concrete Security Against Generic Attacks}
\label{sec:proof-concrete}

\Cref{thm:proof-kylin} is a reduction theorem.  For parameter selection, Kylin
uses a BPORS+FP cover-term proxy for random-function-like instantiations.  Let
$q_s$ be the allowed number of signatures under one key.  For a child PORS tree
with $t$ leaves and $k$ opened leaves, define the fixed-child cover term after
$r$ uses by the inclusion-exclusion formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-bpors-child-cover}
    p_{\mathsf{ch}}(r)
    =
    \sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^i\binom{k}{i}
    \left(\frac{\binom{t-i}{k}}{\binom{t}{k}}\right)^r .
\end{equation}
If the bottom BPORS+FP key has already been used $\gamma$ times, a fixed child
bucket in one coordinate has thinned use count
$R\sim\operatorname{Bin}(\gamma,2^{-B})$.  The one-coordinate averaged child
cover term is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-bpors-thinned-cover}
    \bar p_{\mathsf{ch}}(\gamma)
    =
    \mathbb{E}_{R\sim\operatorname{Bin}(\gamma,2^{-B})}
    \left[p_{\mathsf{ch}}(R)\right].
\end{equation}
The dominant generic forgery probability used in the parameter search is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-generic-prob}
p_{\mathsf{gen}}
=2^{-N}
+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{q_s}
\left(\bar p_{\mathsf{ch}}(\gamma)\right)^K
\binom{q_s}{\gamma}
\left(1-\frac{1}{2^h}\right)^{q_s-\gamma}
2^{-h\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Here $\gamma$ is the number of previous signatures that hit the same hypertree
leaf as the forgery attempt.  Conditioned on this hit count, the first factor
inside the sum is the probability that all $K$ selected BPORS+FP coordinates
are covered by child secrets exposed in previous signatures, after the
$2^{-B}$ bucket thinning in each coordinate.  The leading term
$2^{-N}=2^{-8n}$ captures generic search against an $n$-byte primitive output.
This conservative estimate does not count forced-pruning acceptance as an
independent security gain; $m_{\max}$ is used for fixed-size signatures and
signing-time control.

The corresponding classical and quantum generic bit-security proxies are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proof-bitsec}
    \mathsf{bitsec}_{\mathsf{cl}}=-\log_2 p_{\mathsf{gen}},
    \qquad
    \mathsf{bitsec}_{\mathsf{q}}=-\frac{1}{2}\log_2 p_{\mathsf{gen}},
\end{equation}
where small constant factors inside the logarithm are neglected.  The profile
names are submission names; the table below reports the exponents obtained by
\cref{eq:proof-generic-prob,eq:proof-bitsec} for the frozen parameter file
\texttt{paper/data/para\_kylin\_final\_6profiles.csv}.

\subsection{Relation to the Tight \SPHINCSP Proof}
\label{sec:proof-sphincs-comparison}

The proof above inherits the tight \SPHINCSP structure of~\cite{AC:HulKud22}
and changes two construction-specific parts: the WOTS encoding argument and the
bottom few-time cover calculation.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The \SPHINCSP WOTS+ checksum is replaced by
    \cref{lem:proof-cswots-direction}.  This gives the decreasing chain
    coordinate required for SM-PRE extraction.
    \item The value of $\ell$ is no longer the base-$w$ message length plus a
    checksum length.  Instead, it is the smallest integer satisfying
    \cref{eq:proof-len-condition}.  This changes the concrete target counts
    $q_1,q_2,q_3,q_6$ but not the game sequence.
    \item The SM-UD, SM-TCR, and SM-PRE hybrids for WOTS chains are otherwise
    the same as in WOTS-TW: they depend on chain positions and address
    separation, not on how the message-to-chain-index vector was encoded.
    \item The diverse hypertree heights change the algorithmic loops in
    \texttt{ht\_sign} and \texttt{ht\_verify}, but the reduction uses the total
    height $h$ to bound the number of virtual leaves and targets.
    \item The FORS cover term is replaced by the BPORS+FP cover term in
    \cref{eq:proof-bpors-child-cover,eq:proof-bpors-thinned-cover}.  The game
    structure is the same: after hash-collision and WOTS-forgery events are
    excluded, a remaining bottom-layer forgery must either be covered by
    previously revealed few-time secrets or yield a second child leaf for
    $\hashF$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Other Security Properties}
\label{sec:proof-other-properties}

No additional formal security claims are made in this draft.  In particular,
the current text does not claim dedicated side-channel resistance, perfect
forward security, a quantified multi-key reduction beyond the single-key
target counts above, or a separate theorem for temporary key reuse.

\subsection{Security of the ICCS Hash/XOF Instantiation}
\label{sec:proof-iccs-instantiation}

The packaged NGCC implementation uses the ICCS auxiliary functions
\texttt{sm3hash} and \texttt{pseudohash}, with XOF functionality provided by
\texttt{pseudoXOF}.  At the scheme level, \hashmsg, \PRF, \PRFmsg, \hashF,
\hashH, and \Th are realized through domain-separated wrappers over
\texttt{pseudoXOF} with explicit tags and seed/address separation in the input
layout.

Accordingly, the security claim in this submission is that these instantiated
functions provide the pseudorandomness, target-collision resistance, preimage
resistance, and related tweakable-hash properties required by
\cref{thm:proof-kylin} at the selected parameter sizes.  This document does not
make an additional independent tightness claim about the internals of the ICCS
helper functions beyond that standard generic-analysis interpretation.
